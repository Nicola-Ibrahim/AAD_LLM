\chapter{Introduction}\label{chap:intro}

Continuous optimization serves as an indispensable backbone across modern physical modeling, financial parameter estimation, robotic control, and systems engineering. Traditionally, identifying global minima or maximizing efficiency thresholds in complex engineering systems required domain experts to construct rigid, hand-crafted mathematical search heuristics over decades of trial and error. 

As industrial applications expand in topological complexity and data corruption, this manual engineering paradigm encounters severe scalability limits. This thesis operates at the intersection of continuous black-box optimization, stochastic uncertainty, and Large Language Models (LLMs). By embedding generative foundation models within an autonomous evolutionary feedback loop, this work systematically evaluates how closed-loop AI pipelines adapt programmatic code structures to survive persistent statistical noise, transitioning Automated Algorithm Design from idealistic, noiseless benchmarks into turbulent, non-deterministic operational environments.

%-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Scalability Limits}\label{sec:intro_solvers}

Historically, the engineering of efficient continuous optimization algorithms produced a vast array of general-purpose solvers derived from rigorous mathematical formulations and empirical refining \cite{Nelder1965, Hansen2001}. These efforts yielded widely deployed frameworks ranging from classical gradient-based methods to popular derivative-free heuristics, most notably the Nelder-Mead simplex algorithm \cite{Nelder1965}, Differential Evolution (DE) \cite{Storn1997}, Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) \cite{Kennedy1995}, and the Covariance Matrix Adaptation Evolution Strategy (CMA-ES) \cite{Hansen2001}.

In continuous black-box scenarios, search heuristics aim to locate the global minimum of an objective function $f: \mathbb{R}^D \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ where analytical gradients and internal mathematical structures remain completely inaccessible, leaving point sampling as the sole mechanism for information retrieval. Given the immense topological diversity of real-world fitness landscapes—characterized by high conditioning, non-separability, and multi-modality—the No Free Lunch theorem dictates that no single general-purpose solver can achieve optimal performance across all problem instances without specialized adaptation \cite{Burke2013}. Custom-tailoring unique algorithmic logic or tuning parameters for every distinct application represents a severe engineering bottleneck, as manual design remains labor-intensive, domain-specific, and unscalable \cite{Burke2013}.


%-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Industrial Motivation}\label{sec:intro_motivation}

The theoretical limitations of hand-crafted optimization solvers carry profound implications across real-world industrial engineering applications. In high-precision domains—such as electro-chemical battery parameter estimation, structural finite-element model updating, dynamic robotic calibration, and power grid state tracking—optimization algorithms rarely evaluate clean, closed-form mathematical equations. Instead, objective evaluations are derived directly from physical hardware sensors, empirical measurement apparatuses, or computationally expensive physical simulations.

In practical industrial settings, physical measurements are persistently corrupted by external electromagnetic interference, thermal drift, sensor quantization limitations, and environmental turbulence. When standard human-engineered metaheuristics (such as CMA-ES, Particle Swarm Optimization, or Differential Evolution) traverse these sensor-corrupted landscapes, localized noise misleads search mechanisms. Because ranking-based metaheuristics rely on sorting candidate population members to determine search trajectories, a fundamentally poor physical parameter configuration may yield a temporarily superior objective value over a true optimal candidate purely due to a positive noise anomaly. Hand-crafted heuristics misinterpret these deceptive local spikes as structural landscape improvements, causing algorithms to incorrectly adjust search steps toward false gradients. Standard human-engineered workarounds—such as static sample averaging or scaling up population sizes—over-consume computational budgets without guaranteeing convergence. These physical and computational limits create an active industrial mandate to evaluate whether Automated Algorithm Design frameworks can autonomously synthesize specialized, noise-resilient search rules capable of dynamically filtering sensor deception.

\section{The Core Research Gap}\label{sec:intro_gap}

To automate the discovery of specialized search strategies and eliminate human design bottlenecks, early hyper-heuristic and Automated Algorithm Design (AAD) frameworks relied heavily on Genetic Programming to combine human-authored heuristic components \cite{Burke2013, vanRijn2016}. However, because traditional generation methodologies depended on predefined, human-designed building blocks, their exploration was frequently restricted by rigid search space boundaries and domain-specific primitive choices \cite{Burke2013}.

Recently, AAD has experienced a major resurgence driven by Large Language Models (LLMs). Rather than relying on static search templates or direct coordinate sampling \cite{Lange2024}, modern AAD frameworks embed foundation models within structured, closed-loop evolutionary environments capable of autonomously writing, testing, and refining complete, executable source code structures that rival or surpass traditional human-engineered baselines \cite{Romera2023}. To operationalize this autonomous loop, this research leverages the Large Language Model Evolutionary Algorithm (LLaMEA) framework \cite{LLaMEA2024}. LLaMEA prompts a generative model to output fully functional Python optimization classes, executes the generated code within a validation sandbox against continuous benchmarks, and iteratively feeds raw execution metrics and compiler tracebacks back into the LLM's context window to drive self-directed mutation and logical debugging.

Despite breakthroughs across modern AAD frameworks—such as FunSearch \cite{Romera2023}, Evolution of Heuristics (EoH) \cite{Liu2024}, and LLaMEA \cite{LLaMEA2024}—existing literature suffers from a critical structural limitation: **evaluations are conducted almost exclusively under complete determinism.** Current state-of-the-art frameworks benchmark generated algorithms against clean continuous function suites or discrete combinatorial problems. When an LLM evolves code strictly in noiseless context windows, it synthesizes search logic optimized for exact mathematical precision. However, when an algorithm optimized under deterministic expectations encounters persistent statistical noise during execution, its selection tracking inevitably decays due to severe ranking corruption. A detailed review of these failure mechanics and noise taxonomies is provided in Chapter~\ref{chap:research}.

Currently, it remains entirely unknown whether closed-loop evolutionary pressure within an LLM context window can compel a foundation model to discover specialized uncertainty-mitigation mechanics, whether cost-effective language models possess sufficient programmatic reasoning to synthesize these strategies compared to commercial baselines, and whether algorithms evolved in stochastic sandboxes achieve cross-environment generalizability when deployed back onto unseen noisy or deterministic landscapes. This thesis directly targets this unaddressed boundary layer, evaluating the performance and adaptive capacity of LLaMEA when deployed across continuous black-box optimization landscapes corrupted by persistent statistical noise.

Figure~\ref{fig:intro_paradigm} illustrates the generalized closed-loop paradigm of Automated Algorithm Design (AAD) evaluated in this thesis. The architecture operates as an autonomous evolutionary discovery loop structured across three core stages: (1) a Large Language Model acting as a generative coding engine that synthesizes executable candidate source code based on domain specifications, (2) an isolated execution sandbox that runs candidate algorithms against target problem instances, and (3) an assessor module that evaluates quantitative performance metrics and extracts diagnostic runtime tracebacks. Across iterative generations $1 \dots N-1$, execution feedback is passed back into the LLM's context window to guide reflective self-debugging and mutation, culminating in an evolved, specialized optimization heuristic upon termination.

\section{Research Objectives}\label{sec:intro_objectives}

The primary aim of this thesis is to evaluate the capacity of Large Language Models (LLMs) to autonomously synthesize specialized optimization heuristics capable of surviving stochastic fitness environments. To bridge the gap between deterministic Automated Algorithm Design (AAD) frameworks and noisy real-world applications, this research is structured around three core objectives and their corresponding research questions.


\subsection*{Objective 1: Formulating a Stochastic Evaluation Sandbox}
To test the adaptive limits of a generative language model, the execution environment must accurately reflect non-deterministic conditions by transforming deterministic benchmark functions into noisy problem spaces. Standard AAD frameworks evaluate generated code exclusively against clean theoretical baselines. This objective addresses the foundational environment question:

\begin{quote}
\textbf{RQ1: How can continuous optimization benchmarks be systematically extended to simulate stochastic landscapes?}
\end{quote}

To address this challenge, this phase centers on the engineering and implementation of a non-deterministic wrapper layer over continuous benchmark functions. Grounded in established optimization literature \cite{Jin2005, Hansen2009Noisy}, this phase evaluates how the systematic application of parameter-controlled statistical noise distributions deforms baseline evaluations. The primary goal is to establish a repeatable, secure evaluation harness that effectively masks exact analytical metrics and subjects an automated agent to corrupted fitness values, thereby creating the necessary environmental pressure to test for genuine stochastic adaptation in language model code synthesis.


\subsection*{Objective 2: Autonomous Synthesis of Noise-Resilient Heuristics}
While recent literature confirms that automated frameworks can generate highly competitive algorithms for clean, noiseless functions, it remains an open theoretical question whether these generative loops can adapt to stochastic disruption. Evaluating whether cost-effective, open-weights language models can match the generative performance of high-capability commercial models addresses a critical computational and practical constraint in evolutionary code generation:

\begin{quote}
\textbf{RQ2: Can Large Language Models, across open-weights and proprietary architectures, autonomously synthesize optimization heuristics that are resilient to noise?}
\end{quote}

To achieve this objective, the LLaMEA evolutionary framework is deployed across a representative set of continuous mathematical landscapes using both lightweight, cost-effective language models and high-capability commercial baselines. This phase leverages closed-loop evolutionary feedback to evaluate how language models adapt executable code structures when exposed to non-deterministic landscape evaluations. By analyzing how different model architectures evolve Python optimization scripts across multiple generations, this phase investigates whether closed-loop environmental pressures can drive language models to independently discover, program, and refine specialized uncertainty-handling search logic.


\subsection*{Objective 3: Empirical Benchmarking and Cross-Environment Analysis}
Validating the performance profiles of LLM-generated algorithms under uncertainty requires isolating how their robustness shifts across varied problem conditions. This evaluation drives the final comparative question:

\begin{quote}
\textbf{RQ3: How does the cross-environment robustness of noise-evolved heuristics compare against noiseless baselines?}
\end{quote}

To validate the significance of noise-aware evolution, this thesis establishes a comparative benchmarking framework that evaluates runtime convergence rates and solution quality within corrupted environments. Evolved champion algorithms are evaluated against control groups synthesized under clean conditions as well as traditional baselines. Testing these suites across identical stochastic setups allows this objective to systematically measure the exact performance differential yielded by exposing the generative model to persistent uncertainty. Finally, this phase culminates in a comprehensive performance analysis, directly comparing empirical results with findings from baseline literature operating under clean criteria to isolate the structural boundaries an automated framework independently exhibits under severe uncertainty.

\section{Summary of Key Contributions}\label{sec:intro_contributions}

This thesis establishes an empirical computer science framework to systematically evaluate Automated Algorithm Design (AAD) under non-deterministic conditions. Rather than assuming ideal deterministic execution, this work focuses on the engineering, execution, and analysis of LLM-driven algorithm discovery under controlled fitness noise.

To address the identified research gap, the core contributions delivered by this thesis are as follows:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Stochastic Benchmark Sandbox Environment:} The architectural design and implementation of an extensible, non-deterministic evaluation sandbox wrapping continuous BBOB benchmark functions. This sandbox injects parameter-controlled statistical noise directly into objective evaluations, establishing a standardized software harness for testing search heuristics under fitness space uncertainty.
    
    \item \textbf{Closed-Loop Autonomous Discovery Pipeline:} The deployment and execution of a closed-loop evolutionary discovery pipeline extending the LLaMEA architecture into stochastic environments. This contribution establishes a framework for passing execution tracebacks and performance feedback back to Large Language Models (across open-weights and proprietary architectures) to drive automated code mutation under uncertainty.
    
    \item \textbf{Comprehensive Empirical Robustness Evaluation:} A systematic empirical benchmark evaluating LLM-generated heuristics against clean-trained control groups and classical metaheuristic baselines (such as CMA-ES and Differential Evolution). This analysis provides a structured comparison of solution quality, runtime convergence, and programmatic code traits synthesized under non-deterministic conditions.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Scope and Limitations}\label{sec:intro_scope}

To ensure rigorous experimental control and maintain computational feasibility, the operational scope of this thesis is defined by the following explicit boundaries:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Problem Domain:} This research focuses exclusively on continuous, single-objective black-box optimization problems where $f: \mathbb{R}^D \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$. To maintain computational viability and prevent algorithmic overfitting during the expensive LLM generation loop, evaluation is constrained to a representative gauntlet drawn from the Black-Box Optimization Benchmark (BBOB) suite. Rather than evaluating candidate algorithms against the full 24-function suite simultaneously, evolution is driven by selecting one representative function from each of the five primary BBOB topological classes (e.g., separable, ill-conditioned, multi-modal). Discrete combinatorial optimization, multi-objective trade-offs, and constrained optimization problems remain strictly outside the scope of this evaluation.
    
    \item \textbf{Noise Taxonomy:} Environmental uncertainty is bounded strictly to stochastic noise operating within the objective fitness space. Rather than utilizing unbounded additive noise—which can either overwhelm flat landscapes or disappear on steep topologies—this thesis implements a custom \textit{landscape-scaled additive Gaussian noise} model. Prior to optimization, a baseline landscape scale $S$ is estimated by sampling the mean absolute deviation of uniformly distributed points from the global optimum. Additive Gaussian noise is then injected as $\mathcal{N}(0, (\gamma \cdot S)^2)$, where $\gamma$ is a configurable standard deviation factor. This ensures noise magnitude remains proportional to the underlying function's topology. Parameter uncertainty (perturbation of candidate vectors $x$) and time-varying dynamic environments are excluded to cleanly isolate the impact of fitness corruption.
    
    \item \textbf{Evaluation Limits:} Evolutionary code discovery runs are governed by predefined function evaluation budgets per candidate algorithm and capped generational limits per LLaMEA execution cycle. This ensures standardized comparisons across algorithms while strictly managing the financial token costs and latency associated with language model API interactions.
\end{itemize}
