\chapter{Introduction}\label{chap:intro}

To properly contextualize the operational envelope of this research, it is necessary to chart the evolution of continuous optimization methodologies alongside recent breakthroughs in artificial intelligence and Automated Algorithm Design (AAD). Continuous optimization serves as an indispensable foundation across complex physical modeling, financial parameter estimation, robotic control, and systems engineering. Historically, identifying global minima or maximizing engineering efficiency thresholds required human domain experts to construct rigid, hand-crafted mathematical search heuristics over decades of manual trial and error. 

However, as industrial applications increase in topological complexity and data corruption, this manual engineering loop encounters severe scalability limitations. This thesis operates at the intersection of continuous black-box optimization, stochastic uncertainty, and Large Language Models (LLMs). By embedding generative foundation models within an autonomous evolutionary feedback loop, this work systematically evaluates how closed-loop AI pipelines adapt programmatic code structures to survive persistent statistical noise, transitioning Automated Algorithm Design from idealistic noiseless benchmarks to turbulent, non-deterministic operational environments.

%-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Background and Context}\label{sec:intro_bg}

Historically, the engineering of efficient continuous optimization algorithms required rigid mathematical derivations and extensive manual trial-and-error processes conducted by human experts over decades \cite{Nelder1965, Hansen2001}. This effort produced a vast array of general-purpose solvers, ranging from classical gradient-based methods (such as Gradient Descent and Quasi-Newton algorithms) to popular derivative-free heuristics, most notably the Nelder-Mead simplex algorithm \cite{Nelder1965}, Differential Evolution (DE) \cite{Storn1997}, Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) \cite{Kennedy1995}, and the Covariance Matrix Adaptation Evolution Strategy (CMA-ES) \cite{Hansen2001}. 

However, continuous black-box optimization focuses on identifying the global minimum of a function $f: \mathbb{R}^D \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ where analytical gradients and internal mathematical structures are completely inaccessible, leaving point sampling as the sole mechanism for information retrieval. Due to the immense topological diversity of real-world fitness landscapes—characterized by high conditioning, non-separability, and multi-modality—the No Free Lunch theorem dictates that no single general-purpose solver can achieve optimal performance across all problem instances without specialized adaptation \cite{Burke2013}. Custom-tailoring unique algorithmic logic or tuning parameters for every distinct application represents a severe engineering bottleneck, as manual design is heavily labor-intensive, domain-specific, and unscalable \cite{Burke2013}.


%-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Industrial Motivation}\label{sec:intro_motivation}

The theoretical limitations of hand-crafted optimization solvers carry profound implications across real-world industrial engineering applications. In high-precision domains—such as electro-chemical battery parameter estimation, structural finite-element model updating, dynamic robotic calibration, and power grid state tracking—optimization algorithms rarely evaluate clean, closed-form mathematical equations. Instead, objective evaluations are derived directly from physical hardware sensors, empirical measurement apparatuses, or computationally expensive physical simulations \cite{Plett2004, Mottershead1993}.

In practical industrial settings, these physical measurements are persistently corrupted by external electromagnetic interference, thermal drift, sensor quantization limitations, and environmental turbulence \cite{Xiong2018}. A prominent example is the parameter identification of Lithium-ion battery packs in Electric Vehicles (EVs). Engineers utilize metaheuristic algorithms to extract dynamic physical parameters—such as internal ohmic resistance ($R_0$) and polarization capacitance ($C_{pa}$)—by minimizing the root-mean-square error between simulated model outputs and measured terminal voltage traces \cite{Plett2004, Xiong2018}. However, because operational battery sensors incur high-frequency current ripples and dynamic temperature shifts, the observed fitness value $f_{\text{observed}}(x)$ is never deterministic. Instead, the true time-invariant system response $f(x)$ is masked by a stochastic error component $\epsilon$, rendering the measured evaluation a random variable centered around the underlying true performance:
\begin{equation}
    f_{\text{observed}}(x) = f(x) + \epsilon, \quad \epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)
\end{equation}

When standard human-engineered metaheuristics (such as CMA-ES, Particle Swarm Optimization, or Differential Evolution) traverse these sensor-corrupted landscapes, localized noise destroys their path convergence properties \cite{Jin2005, Marwala2010}. Because ranking-based metaheuristics rely on sorting candidate population members to determine search trajectories, a fundamentally poor physical parameter configuration $x_{\text{poor}}$ may yield a temporarily superior objective value over a true optimal candidate $x_{\text{opt}}$ purely due to a positive statistical anomaly ($\epsilon_{\text{spike}}$). Hand-crafted heuristics misinterpret these deceptive local spikes as structural landscape improvements, causing algorithms to incorrectly update covariance matrices, step sizes, or velocity vectors toward false gradients \cite{Hansen2009Noisy, Jin2005}. This vulnerability is even more severe in classical gradient-based algorithms; because these systems rely entirely on computing exact analytical or finite-difference derivatives, even minor statistical noise completely corrupts gradient estimation vectors, leading to immediate algorithmic failure \cite{Sarafian2024}.

Similar challenges plague mechanical vibration engineering, where structural parameters (such as elastic moduli and damping coefficients) are updated by fitting finite-element models against noisy accelerometer data \cite{Mottershead1993, Marwala2010}. Traditional human-engineered workarounds—such as static sample averaging or increasing population sizes—dramatically over-consume computational budgets and degrade optimization throughput without guaranteeing convergence \cite{Jin2005}. Furthermore, pre-runtime hyperparameter tuning fails to resolve this bottleneck, as fixed static configurations cannot dynamically react to localized noise fluctuations across heterogeneous search spaces. These physical and computational limits create an active industrial mandate to evaluate whether Automated Algorithm Design frameworks can autonomously discover specialized, noise-resilient search rules capable of dynamically filtering sensor deception without requiring manual mathematical derivation.

\section{The Core Research Gap}\label{sec:intro_gap}

To automate the discovery of specialized search strategies and eliminate human design bottlenecks, techniques such as hyperparameter tuning, automated algorithm selection, and traditional Automated Algorithm Design (AAD) were developed long before the advent of deep learning \cite{vanRijn2016}. Early AAD frameworks relied primarily on Genetic Programming or modular human-authored search spaces \cite{Burke2013}. However, the immense combinatorial breadth of programmatic space and the rigid constraints of predefined search blocks frequently restricted true algorithmic discovery.

Recently, AAD in particular has experienced a major resurgence driven by Large Language Models (LLMs). Rather than relying on rigid, predefined search templates or direct coordinate sampling \cite{Lange2024}, modern AAD frameworks embed foundation models within structured, closed-loop evolutionary environments capable of autonomously writing, testing, and refining complete executable source code structures that rival or surpass traditional human-engineered baselines \cite{Romera2023}. 

To operationalize this autonomous code-generation loop, this research leverages the Large Language Model Evolutionary Algorithm (LLaMEA) framework \cite{LLaMEA2024}. The system pipeline prompts the generative model to output fully functional Python optimization classes, executes the generated script within a secure validation sandbox against standard mathematical benchmarks, and iteratively feeds raw execution metrics and compiler tracebacks directly back into the LLM's context window to drive self-directed mutation and logical debugging. 

Despite recent breakthroughs in Large Language Model-driven Automated Algorithm Design—such as FunSearch \cite{Romera2023}, Evolution of Heuristics (EoH) \cite{Liu2024}, and LLaMEA \cite{LLaMEA2024}—existing literature suffers from a critical structural limitation: evaluations are conducted almost exclusively under complete determinism. Current state-of-the-art AAD frameworks benchmark generated algorithms against clean continuous function suites (such as the standard noiseless BBOB benchmark \cite{Hansen2010}) or discrete combinatorial problems (such as the Traveling Salesperson Problem \cite{AEL2024}). When an LLM evolves code in these noiseless context windows, it learns logic optimized for exact mathematical precision. However, if an automated code class optimized strictly under deterministic expectations experiences persistent statistical noise during execution, its selection tracking inevitably decays due to ranking corruption \cite{Jin2005}.

Currently, it remains entirely unknown whether closed-loop evolutionary pressure within an LLM context window can compel a foundation model to discover specialized uncertainty-mitigation mechanics, whether cost-effective language models possess sufficient programmatic reasoning to synthesize these strategies compared to commercial baselines, and whether algorithms evolved in stochastic sandboxes achieve cross-environment generalizability when deployed back onto unseen noisy or deterministic landscapes. This thesis directly targets this unaddressed boundary layer, evaluating the performance and adaptive capacity of LLaMEA when deployed across continuous black-box optimization landscapes corrupted by persistent statistical noise.

Figure~\ref{fig:intro_paradigm} illustrates the high-level operational paradigm evaluated in this thesis. The framework establishes a closed-loop evolutionary process comprising three core stages: (1) a Large Language Model acting as a programmatic code generator to output candidate Python optimization classes, (2) a non-deterministic evaluation sandbox that corrupts benchmark functions with controlled statistical noise distributions, and (3) an assessor module that extracts execution metrics (AOCC) and runtime tracebacks, passing them directly back into the LLM's context window to drive self-debugging and stochastic adaptation.

\section{Research Objectives and Questions}\label{sec:intro_objectives}

The primary aim of this thesis is to evaluate the capacity of Large Language Models (LLMs) to autonomously synthesize specialized optimization heuristics that are mathematically resilient to stochastic environments. To systematically bridge the gap between existing deterministic Automated Algorithm Design (AAD) frameworks and noisy real-world applications, this research is structured around three core objectives and their corresponding research questions.

\textbf{Objective 1: Formulating a Stochastic Evaluation Sandbox.} To test the adaptive limits of an LLM, the execution environment must accurately reflect non-deterministic conditions by transforming deterministic benchmark functions into noisy problem spaces. Currently, standard AAD frameworks evaluate generated code exclusively against clean theoretical baselines. This objective addresses the foundational environment question:

\begin{quote}
\textbf{RQ1: How can continuous optimization benchmarks be systematically extended to simulate stochastic landscapes?}
\end{quote}

To address this challenge, this objective centers on the engineering and implementation of a non-deterministic wrapper layer over continuous benchmark functions. Grounded in established optimization literature \cite{Jin2005, Hansen2009Noisy}, this phase focuses on evaluating how the systematic application of a parameter-controlled statistical noise distribution deforms baseline evaluations. The primary goal is to establish a repeatable, secure evaluation harness that effectively masks exact analytical metrics and subjects an automated agent to corrupted fitness values, thereby creating the necessary environmental pressure to test for genuine stochastic adaptation in language model code synthesis.

\textbf{Objective 2: Autonomous Synthesis of Noise-Resilient Heuristics.} While recent literature confirms that automated frameworks can generate highly competitive algorithms for clean, noiseless functions, it remains an open theoretical boundary whether these generative loops can adapt to stochastic disruption. Furthermore, evaluating whether cost-effective, lightweight language models can match the generative performance of high-capability commercial models addresses a critical computational and practical constraint in evolutionary code generation. Consequently, this objective addresses the primary generative capability and cost-efficiency of the framework:

\begin{quote}
\textbf{RQ2: Can Large Language Models, across open-weights and proprietary architectures, autonomously synthesize optimization heuristics that are resilient to noise?}
\end{quote}

To achieve this objective, the LLaMEA evolutionary framework will be deployed across a representative set of continuous mathematical landscapes using both lightweight, cost-effective language models and high-capability commercial baselines. This phase leverages closed-loop evolutionary feedback to evaluate how language models adapt executable code structures when exposed to non-deterministic landscape evaluations. By analyzing how different model architectures evolve Python optimization scripts across multiple generations, this phase aims to investigate whether closed-loop environmental pressures can drive language models to independently discover, program, and refine specialized uncertainty-handling search logic.

\textbf{Objective 3: Empirical Benchmarking and Cross-Environment Analysis.} It is critical to formally validate the performance profiles of these LLM-generated algorithms under uncertainty and isolate how their robustness shifts across varied problem conditions. This evaluation drives the final comparative question:

\begin{quote}
\textbf{RQ3: How does the cross-environment robustness of noise-evolved heuristics compare against noiseless baselines?}
\end{quote}

To validate the significance of noise-aware evolution, we will establish a rigorous comparative benchmarking framework that evaluates runtime convergence rates and solution quality within corrupted environments. Evolved champion algorithms will be evaluated against control groups synthesized under clean conditions as well as traditional baselines. Testing these suites across identical stochastic setups allows this objective to systematically measure the exact performance differential yielded by exposing the generative model to persistent uncertainty. Finally, this phase will culminate in a comprehensive performance analysis, directly comparing our empirical results with findings from baseline literature operating under clean, noiseless criteria to isolate the structural and behavioral boundaries an automated framework independently exhibits under severe uncertainty.

\section{Summary of Key Contributions}\label{sec:intro_contributions}

This thesis establishes a fundamental, benchmark-driven computer science evaluation to isolate the capabilities of Automated Algorithm Design under uncertainty. By systematically corrupting synthetic continuous math landscapes with a parameter-controlled statistical noise distribution, we establish a clean, repeatable evaluation loop to test the adaptive limits of LLM-driven code evolution.

To systematically resolve the identified research gaps, the core contributions of this work are summarized as follows:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Stochastic Benchmark Sandbox Wrapper:} The design and implementation of an extensible, non-deterministic evaluation wrapper over continuous benchmark functions. This sandbox injects controlled statistical noise directly into the fitness space, creating a repeatable environment that models sensor uncertainty and forces algorithms to operate without exact gradient information.
    \item \textbf{Autonomous Generation of Noise-Resilient Heuristics:} The execution of a closed-loop evolutionary system leveraging the LLaMEA architecture under persistent statistical noise. This contribution provides empirical proof of whether language models can autonomously transition from deterministic search assumptions to writing, debugging, and refining specialized uncertainty-handling code traits in Python.
    \item \textbf{Comparative Model Efficiency and Cross-Environment Report:} A comprehensive empirical benchmark evaluating noise-trained champion algorithms against clean-trained controls, traditional metaheuristic baselines (such as CMA-ES and Differential Evolution), and across both cost-effective open models and commercial foundation models. This white-box analysis isolates the structural code traits independently invented by AI to prevent deceptive convergence.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Scope and Limitations}\label{sec:intro_scope}

To ensure rigorous experimental control and maintain computational feasibility, the operational scope of this thesis is defined by the following explicit boundaries:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Problem Domain:} This research focuses exclusively on continuous, single-objective black-box optimization problems where $f: \mathbb{R}^D \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$. Discrete combinatorial optimization, multi-objective trade-off spaces, and constrained optimization problems are outside the scope of this evaluation.
    \item \textbf{Noise Taxonomy:} Environmental uncertainty is bounded strictly to stochastic noise operating within the fitness space (objective evaluations). Parameter uncertainty (perturbation of candidate vectors $x$) and time-varying topological drift are excluded to isolate the impact of corrupted ranking feedback.
    \item \textbf{Evaluation and Compute Limits:} Evolutionary code discovery runs are governed by predefined function evaluation budgets per candidate algorithm and capped generation limits per LLaMEA execution cycle. This ensures standardized comparisons while managing token costs associated with language model API interactions.
\end{itemize}
