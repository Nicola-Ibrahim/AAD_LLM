\chapter{Methodology}\label{chap:methodology}

Evolutionary loops operating over stochastic fitness evaluations suffer from deceptive selection signals. When objective returns carry variance, candidate algorithms easily exploit lucky evaluation samples, which stalls code generation routines that rely on reliable directional feedback. Resolving this failure mode requires an execution environment that isolates stochastic noise during candidate evaluation while maintaining clean progress metrics for outer-loop selection.

This chapter establishes the mathematical specifications, execution sandboxing, and statistical protocols of the automated algorithm design system. First, we formalize the optimality-gap heteroscedastic noise model and trace its behavior across representative continuous topologies. We then define the dual-evaluation decoupling layer that isolates candidate search logic from diagnostic telemetry, followed by the Abstract Syntax Tree (AST) validation rules, execution timeouts, and traceback recovery pipelines. Subsequent sections detail the evolutionary selection operators, the stochastic parent resampling policy ($K=3$), and the prompt scaffolding configurations evaluated in our experiments. We conclude by specifying the orchestration lifecycle and the non-parametric statistical hypothesis testing protocol used for empirical verification.

\section{Noise Modeling}\label{sec:noise_modeling}

Automated synthesis of noise-resilient continuous heuristics requires benchmarking against stochastic fitness surfaces \cite{Jin2005}. We use the continuous Black-Box Optimization Benchmark (BBOB) suite as our base testbed \cite{Hansen2010}. Because standard BBOB functions evaluate deterministically, the execution wrapper must inject controlled variance directly at the interface level.

In experimental optimization and physical engineering domains---such as chemical process control, wind turbine aerodynamics, and physical sensor telemetry---observed measurement uncertainty rarely stems from a single isolated source. Instead, it arises from the continuous aggregation of countless independent micro-disturbances. By the Central Limit Theorem (CLT), the sum of these independent stochastic errors naturally converges to a normal distribution, establishing additive Gaussian noise $\mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)$ as the most physically realistic model for continuous search environments \cite{Shir2012, Spall2003}.

Beyond physical realism, zero-mean Gaussian perturbations guarantee an unbiased mathematical expectation ($\mathbb{E}[z] = 0$). Consequently, the expected value of any corrupted observation strictly equals the underlying true objective elevation: $\mathbb{E}[f_{\text{noisy}}(x)] = f_{\text{clean}}(x)$ \cite{Spall2003}. This unbiased property is vital for Automated Algorithm Design: it ensures that if the language model successfully synthesizes noise-mitigation mechanics---such as Monte Carlo sample averaging ($\frac{1}{K} \sum_{i=1}^K f_{\text{noisy}}(x_i)$) or momentum-based filtering---the heuristic will consistently converge toward the true uncorrupted mean without accumulating systematic mathematical bias.

In the optimization literature, Gaussian uncertainty is commonly formulated either as homoscedastic static additive noise \cite{Jin2005}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:static_noise_lit}
    f_{\text{noisy}}(x) = f_{\text{clean}}(x) + \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)
\end{equation}
or through point-wise multiplicative variance \cite{Jin2005}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:naive_multiplicative_lit}
    f_{\text{noisy}}(x) = f_{\text{clean}}(x) \cdot \left(1 + \mathcal{N}(0, \text{CV}^2)\right)
\end{equation}
where $x \in \mathbb{R}^D$ denotes a $D$-dimensional coordinate vector, $f_{\text{clean}}(x) \in \mathbb{R}$ is the true objective value, $\sigma^2 \in \mathbb{R}^+$ is a fixed variance, $\text{CV} \in \mathbb{R}^+$ is the dimensionless Coefficient of Variation ($\text{CV} = \sigma / \mu$), and $f_{\text{noisy}}(x) \in \mathbb{R}$ represents the corrupted signal returned to the optimizer.

Both formulations break down across heterogeneous benchmark suites \cite{Hansen2010}. Homoscedastic additive noise (Equation~\ref{eq:static_noise_lit}) applies constant variance regardless of objective magnitude. Near the optimum where objective values drop to $10^{-5}$, this constant variance produces an overwhelmingly low Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR) that obliterates descent gradients. Conversely, on steep sections where objective values exceed $10^6$, the identical additive variance becomes negligible, allowing optimizers to converge without developing explicit noise-filtering routines \cite{Hansen2010}.

Multiplicative noise (Equation~\ref{eq:naive_multiplicative_lit}) introduces a different artifact by scaling variance directly against raw elevation $f_{\text{clean}}(x)$. This formulation includes any vertical translation $f(x^*)$ added to the global minimum. Adding a vertical offset $g(x) = f(x) + 10\,000$ scales the variance by orders of magnitude, corrupting the local basin structure without altering the underlying spatial topography.

BBOB function $f_{21}$ (Gallagher's Gaussian 101 Peaks) highlights the ranking vulnerability of naive multiplicative and homoscedastic models. The global minimum sits at an analytical offset of $f(x^*) \approx 40.78$, across a total dynamic range of roughly 50 units. Under naive multiplicative noise with $\text{CV} = 0.05$, a coordinate evaluated near the global optimum at $f_{\text{clean}}(x) = 41.0$ yields a standard deviation of:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:f21_naive_std}
    \sigma = 0.05 \cdot 41.0 = 2.05
\end{equation}
This deviation spans $4\%$ of the total function range. Because peak elevation gaps in $f_{21}$ span only $2$ to $5$ units, this variance erases local basin boundaries. Evaluating adjacent coordinates $x_a$ ($f_{\text{clean}} = 41.5$) and $x_b$ ($f_{\text{clean}} = 42.0$) produces heavily overlapping distributions: $f_{\text{noisy}}(x_a) \sim \mathcal{N}(41.5, 2.075^2)$ and $f_{\text{noisy}}(x_b) \sim \mathcal{N}(42.0, 2.1^2)$. Stochastic draws frequently invert the true ranking ($f_{\text{noisy}}(x_a) > f_{\text{noisy}}(x_b)$), depriving search routines of usable directional signals.

We resolve these discrepancies using a \textbf{Heteroscedastic Optimality-Gap Noise Model}. Rather than scaling variance against arbitrary raw function values, variance is tied directly to the distance from the global minimum. For any coordinate $x_k \in \mathbb{R}^D$ queried at step $k$, the local standard deviation $\sigma_{\text{dynamic}}(x_k)$ scales strictly with the absolute optimality gap:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:proportional_dynamic_std}
    \sigma_{\text{dynamic}}(x_k) = \text{CV} \cdot \left| f_{\text{clean}}(x_k) - f(x^*) \right|
\end{equation}
where $\text{CV} \ge 0$ parameterizes the noise severity (corresponding to the standard deviation scale in the execution wrapper), and $\left| f_{\text{clean}}(x_k) - f(x^*) \right|$ is the true spatial error.

The benchmark harness preserves the true minimum $f(x^*)$ internally while hiding it from the generated Python script. At evaluation step $k$, the candidate receives a corrupted scalar observation:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:proportional_gaussian_noise}
    f_{\text{noisy}}(x_k) = f_{\text{clean}}(x_k) + z_k, \quad z_k \sim \mathcal{N}\left(0, \sigma_{\text{dynamic}}^2(x_k)\right)
\end{equation}
where $z_k \in \mathbb{R}$ is an independent Gaussian perturbation sampled with density:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gaussian_pdf_dynamic}
    p(z_k) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi \sigma_{\text{dynamic}}^2(x_k)}} \exp\left( -\frac{z_k^2}{2\sigma_{\text{dynamic}}^2(x_k)} \right)
\end{equation}
Because $p(z_k)$ is symmetric with zero expectation ($\mathbb{E}[z_k] = 0$), the noisy evaluation acts as an unbiased estimator of true fitness:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:unbiased_expectation}
    \mathbb{E}[f_{\text{noisy}}(x_k)] = f_{\text{clean}}(x_k)
\end{equation}

To maintain a strict black-box operational contract, the analytical minimum $f(x^*)$ and the runtime calculation of $\sigma_{\text{dynamic}}(x_k)$ remain strictly encapsulated within the execution wrapper. The candidate heuristic receives only the scalar float $f_{\text{noisy}}(x_k)$, preventing generated algorithms from introspecting the noise envelope or directly reverse-engineering the target coordinate. Furthermore, each query issued to the interface triggers an independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) stochastic sample $z_k$. Re-evaluating the identical coordinate $x_k$ across $K$ consecutive queries yields uncorrelated realizations, allowing candidate heuristics that implement Monte Carlo resampling to suppress effective noise variance proportionally to $1/\sqrt{K}$.

Scaling variance heteroscedastically with the optimality gap provides two core properties for Automated Algorithm Design:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Scale Invariance:} The Signal-to-Noise Ratio remains bounded by $1/\text{CV}$ across varying benchmark ranges, from $f_8$ ($\sim 10^3$) to $f_{21}$ ($\sim 50$), removing the need to calibrate noise hyperparameters per function.
    \item \textbf{Structured Search Difficulty:} Regions far from the target exhibit high variance, while points close to the optimum narrow their variance profiles, matching empirical systems where measurement noise scales with distance from target equilibrium.
\end{enumerate}

Applying this model to $f_{21}$ ($\text{CV} = 0.05$), test coordinates $x_a$ ($f_{\text{clean}} = 41.5$) and $x_b$ ($f_{\text{clean}} = 42.0$) yield standard deviations of:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:f21_gap_std_a}
    \sigma_{\text{dynamic}}(x_a) = 0.05 \cdot |41.5 - 40.78| \approx 0.036
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:f21_gap_std_b}
    \sigma_{\text{dynamic}}(x_b) = 0.05 \cdot |42.0 - 40.78| \approx 0.061
\end{equation}
The resulting distributions, $f_{\text{noisy}}(x_a) \sim \mathcal{N}(41.5, 0.036^2)$ and $f_{\text{noisy}}(x_b) \sim \mathcal{N}(42.0, 0.061^2)$, preserve ranking order between both points and maintain usable descent vectors.

In contrast, the heteroscedastic optimality-gap formulation (Panel C) naturally tightens its variance envelope as coordinates approach the global minimizer $x^*$, preserving basin boundaries and retaining an actionable descent signal toward the global basin.

To evaluate behavior on large-scale, ill-conditioned geometries, consider BBOB function $f_8$ (Rosenbrock). Benchmark instances apply spatial translations $x^* \in \mathbb{R}^D$ and orthogonal rotation matrices $R \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times D}$ to eliminate coordinate alignment \cite{Hansen2010}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bbob_f8_formula}
    f_8(x) = \sum_{i=1}^{D-1} \left[ 100 \cdot \left( y_{i+1} - y_i^2 \right)^2 + (1 - y_i)^2 \right] + f_{\text{opt}}
\end{equation}
where $y = \max\left(1, \frac{\sqrt{D}}{8}\right) R (x - x^*) + \mathbf{1}$, $\mathbf{1} \in \mathbb{R}^D$ is a vector of ones, and $f_{\text{opt}} \in \mathbb{R}$ is the target minimum.

The term $100 \cdot (y_{i+1} - y_i^2)^2$ forms a steep parabolic valley, pushing boundary deviations $f_{\text{clean}}(x_k) - f_{\text{opt}}$ up to $10^3$. Setting $\text{CV} = 0.1$, exploration along domain boundaries where $f_{\text{clean}}(x_k) - f_{\text{opt}} \approx 10^3$ sets the standard deviation to:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:f8_dynamic_std_boundary}
    \sigma_{\text{dynamic}}(x_k) = 0.1 \cdot 10^3 = 10^2
\end{equation}
As search vectors enter the valley floor where $f_{\text{clean}}(x_k) - f_{\text{opt}} \approx 10^1$, the standard deviation drops proportionally:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:f8_dynamic_std_valley}
    \sigma_{\text{dynamic}}(x_k) = 0.1 \cdot 10^1 = 10^0
\end{equation}
By comparison, evaluating the isotropic sphere function $f_1(x) = \|R(x - x^*)\|^2 + f_{\text{opt}}$ near domain boundaries produces objective differences of $f_{\text{clean}}(x_k) - f_{\text{opt}} \approx 10^1$, yielding $\sigma_{\text{dynamic}} \approx 10^0$. Sampling noise through Equation~\ref{eq:proportional_gaussian_noise} yields perturbations $z_k \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_{\text{dynamic}}^2(x_k))$ that maintain equivalent relative SNR across diverse topographies.

The heteroscedastic optimality-gap formulation (Panel C) scales variance proportionally with objective magnitude across the entire coordinate range, maintaining balanced search difficulty from initial exploration down to local convergence.

\section{Evaluator Architecture}\label{sec:method_decoupling}

The evaluation harness decouples the corrupted fitness returned to the candidate optimizer from the uncorrupted metric recorded by the meta-driver. Candidate algorithms require stochastic feedback $f_{\text{noisy}}(x_k)$ to develop internal variance-reduction routines. Direct logging of noisy evaluations corrupts empirical benchmarking: an optimizer drawing an extreme negative perturbation ($z_k \ll 0$) registers an artificial descent step without approaching the minimizer. The search routine consumes only $f_{\text{noisy}}(x_k)$, whereas the background harness records the true spatial error:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:clean_distance_metric}
\Delta f(x_k) = \left| f_{\text{clean}}(x_k) - f(x^*) \right|
\end{equation}
where $f(x^*) \in \mathbb{R}$ denotes the known global minimum of the benchmark instance.

Candidate optimizers run as self-contained Python classes constrained by an operational black-box interface:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:candidate_interface_call}
\mathcal{H}: (\mathcal{O}_{\text{sandbox}}, B, D, \mathbf{lb}, \mathbf{ub}) \longmapsto (\mathbf{x}^*, y^*)
\end{equation}
In this contract, $\mathcal{O}_{\text{sandbox}}$ wraps the objective function, $B = 100\,000$ sets the function call budget, $D \in \mathbb{N}^+$ denotes problem dimension, and $\mathbf{lb}, \mathbf{ub} \in \mathbb{R}^D$ define domain boundaries ($[-5.0, 5.0]^D$).

At step $k \in \{1 \dots B\}$, query vector $x_k \in \mathbb{R}^D$ passes to the wrapper, which evaluates the analytical BBOB target $f_{\text{clean}}(x_k)$. The wrapper injects zero-mean heteroscedastic noise $z_k \sim \mathcal{N}\left(0, \sigma_{\text{dynamic}}^2(x_k)\right)$ following Equation~\ref{eq:proportional_dynamic_std}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:wrapper_noise_return}
f_{\text{noisy}}(x_k) = f_{\text{clean}}(x_k) + z_k
\end{equation}
The candidate algorithm has no visibility into $f(x^*)$, $f_{\text{clean}}(x_k)$, or $\sigma_{\text{dynamic}}(x_k)$.

Concurrently, the background harness logs the lowest uncorrupted value found up to step $k$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:best_so_far_trajectory}
\Delta f_{\text{best}}(k) = \min_{j \in \{1 \dots k\}} \left| f_{\text{clean}}(x_j) - f(x^*) \right|, \quad \forall k \in \{1 \dots B\}
\end{equation}
Tracking $\Delta f_{\text{best}}(k)$ isolates true coordinate descent from measurement variance.

When the optimizer exhausts budget $B$, it returns estimated minimizer $\mathbf{x}^* \in \mathbb{R}^D$ alongside its self-reported objective value $y^* \in \mathbb{R}$. The evaluator discards $y^*$ and evaluates $\mathbf{x}^*$ directly on the uncorrupted function:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:oracle_reeval}
y_{\text{clean}} = f_{\text{clean}}(\mathbf{x}^*)
\end{equation}
The evolutionary driver scores algorithm $\mathcal{A}$ by taking the negated logarithm of the lowest uncorrupted error achieved during the run:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:meta_fitness_score}
\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{A}) = -\log_{10}\left( \max\left( \min_{k \in \{1 \dots B\}} \Delta f(x_k), \, \tau_{\text{conv}} \right) \right)
\end{equation}
Target precision threshold $\tau_{\text{conv}} = 10^{-8}$ matches standard BBOB precision limits. This logarithmic transform linearizes convergence differences across orders of magnitude, providing smooth selection feedback for the evolutionary search.

\section{Execution Sandbox}\label{sec:method_sandbox_defenses}

Autonomous code synthesis introduces execution hazards, including process hangs, memory leaks, and invalid system calls. The execution harness isolates, parses, and times each generated script before committing evaluations to the evolutionary archive.

\textbf{AST Parsing and Security Boundaries.}
A regular-expression parser extracts Python code blocks from conversational LLM output. The raw code string passes through an Abstract Syntax Tree (AST) visitor before byte compilation:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Import Whitelisting:} The AST allows standard numerical libraries (\texttt{numpy}, \texttt{scipy}, \texttt{math}) while raising syntax violations on system access modules (\texttt{os}, \texttt{sys}, \texttt{subprocess}, \texttt{shutil}, \texttt{socket}, \texttt{importlib}).
\item \textbf{Built-in Sanitization:} Dynamic execution built-ins (\texttt{eval}, \texttt{exec}, \texttt{open}, \texttt{compile}, \texttt{\_\_import\_\_}) cause immediate rejection during AST traversal.
\item \textbf{Interface Conformance:} The visitor verifies that the script defines the target optimizer class along with the entry methods specified by the API contract.
\end{itemize}
Scripts violating these checks fail before execution.

\textbf{Process Timeouts and Spatial Integrity.}
Synthesized routines risk infinite loops, divergent matrix decompositions, or recursive thrashing. Each candidate executes in a detached worker process governed by a 10.0-second wall-clock timeout ($T_{\text{max}} = 10.0\,\text{s}$). If execution exceeds $T_{\text{max}}$, the parent process issues a \texttt{SIGKILL} and logs a timeout failure.

After execution terminates, a boundary validator checks returned coordinate $\mathbf{x}^*$. The run is discarded if $\mathbf{x}^* \notin [\mathbf{lb}, \mathbf{ub}]^D$ or if numerical overflow produces \texttt{NaN} or \texttt{Inf} coordinates.

\textbf{Traceback Capture and Tiered Penalties.}
Runtime exceptions---such as matrix singularity during covariance updates or array broadcasting mismatches---are caught by the harness wrapper. The system logs the full traceback and assigns a tiered failure score:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:failure_penalties}
\mathcal{F}_{\text{failure}} =
\begin{cases}
-1.0 \times 10^7, & \text{if Execution Timeout } (t_{\text{wall}} > T_{\text{max}}) \\
-1.0 \times 10^8, & \text{if Runtime Numerical or Dimensional Exception} \\
-1.0 \times 10^9, & \text{if AST Validation or Syntax Compilation Failure}
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
This tiered assignment maintains selection pressure toward syntactically valid and executable candidates. The parser strips non-informative frames from the traceback string, isolating the failing line pointer (e.g., \texttt{LinAlgError: Matrix is singular at line 48}). This diagnostic snippet is appended to feedback buffer $\mathcal{T}_{\text{feedback}}$, giving the model actionable debugging information for generation $g+1$.

\section{Evolutionary Code Discovery}\label{sec:method_llamea}

The search engine builds upon the Large Language Model Evolutionary Algorithm (LLaMEA) framework \cite{LLaMEA2024}, treating the language model as a semantic mutation operator.

\textbf{The $(1+1)$ Evolution Loop.}
The pipeline executes a $(1+1)$ Evolution Strategy over $G = 20$ generations. At generation $g \in \{1 \dots G\}$, the model mutates incumbent algorithm $\mathcal{A}_{\text{parent}}$ into a single offspring candidate $\mathcal{A}_{\text{child}}$ conditioned on prompt context $\mathcal{C}_g$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:context_assembly}
\mathcal{C}_g = \left\{ \mathcal{P}_{\text{system}}, \mathcal{H}_{1 \dots g-1}, \mathcal{T}_{\text{feedback}}, \mathcal{I}_{\text{directive}} \right\}
\end{equation}
The context integrates four structural streams:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{System Specification ($\mathcal{P}_{\text{system}}$):} Defines the optimizer base class, evaluation budget ($B = 100\,000$), coordinate dimensionality, and spatial domain limits.
\item \textbf{Historical Archive ($\mathcal{H}_{1 \dots g-1}$):} An indexed list of prior candidate implementations paired with their scalar fitness values $\mathcal{F}$, preventing cycling into previously explored search spaces.
\item \textbf{Incumbent Profile ($\mathcal{T}_{\text{feedback}}$):} Contains the active parent implementation $\mathcal{A}_{\text{parent}}$, its lowest uncorrupted error $\min_k \Delta f(x_k)$, convergence dynamics, and tracebacks generated by recent failed mutations.
\item \textbf{Evolutionary Directive ($\mathcal{I}_{\text{directive}}$):} Instructions prompting the model to diagnose failure modes (such as step-size collapse or variance loss) and synthesize structural improvements.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{False Elitism and Stochastic Parent Resampling.}
Under stochastic noise ($f_{\text{noisy}} = f_{\text{clean}} + z$), standard elitist selection breaks down due to false elitism \cite{Jin2005, Qian2020}. A candidate algorithm drawing an extreme negative perturbation ($z_k \ll 0$) receives an artificially deflated error score. If selected as parent, this over-evaluated candidate sets an unattainable fitness target. Subsequent offspring with superior structural mechanics are rejected because their realistic evaluation passes cannot beat the uncorrupted outlier score of the parent, halting optimization.

To address this vulnerability, the framework applies a Stochastic Parent Resampling Policy. At the end of generation $g$, incumbent parent $\mathcal{A}_{\text{parent}}$ is re-evaluated across $K = 3$ independent stochastic passes using fresh random seeds. The incumbent selection score updates to its sample mean:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:parent_resampling}
\bar{\mathcal{F}}_{\text{parent}} = \frac{1}{K} \sum_{k=1}^{K} \mathcal{F}^{(k)}\left(\mathcal{A}_{\text{parent}}\right)
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{F}^{(k)}(\mathcal{A}_{\text{parent}})$ denotes the scalar score from Equation~\ref{eq:meta_fitness_score} on pass $k$. Averaging over multiple evaluations reduces the influence of transient noise spikes, selecting for heuristics with structural variance tolerance.

\end{enumerate}

\section{System Orchestration}\label{sec:method_orchestration}

The interaction between the heteroscedastic noise model, sandboxed execution, prompt scaffolding, and parent resampling forms a closed-loop search system.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\input{figures/evolutionary_aad_loop}
\caption{System orchestration of the stochastic Automated Algorithm Design loop. Source code flows from the model through AST validation to the execution sandbox. Search decisions consume noisy evaluations $f_{\text{noisy}}(x_k)$, while the background logger records true precision $\Delta f(x_k)$ and applies parent resampling ($K=3$, $\bar{f}_{\text{parent}}$) to update prompt context $\mathcal{C}_g$.}
\label{fig:evolutionary_aad_loop}
\end{figure}

Each generation $g \in \{1 \dots G\}$ proceeds through five phases:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Context Assembly and Generation:} The driver builds prompt packet $\mathcal{C}_g$ (Equation~\ref{eq:context_assembly}) containing the chosen prompt archetype, parent code $\mathcal{A}_{\text{parent}}$, and prior error traces. The model mutates the parent to produce $\mathcal{A}_{\text{child}}$.
\item \textbf{Sanitization and Validation:} The harness extracts the code block using regular expressions and parses the AST. Scripts with unauthorized imports or blacklisted built-ins receive $\mathcal{F} = -10^9$ and terminate immediately.
\item \textbf{Decoupled Execution:} The validated candidate executes inside an isolated worker under budget $B = 100\,000$ and timeout $T_{\text{max}} = 10.0\,\text{s}$. The candidate receives noisy values $f_{\text{noisy}}(x_k)$ (Equation~\ref{eq:wrapper_noise_return}), while the background harness logs uncorrupted spatial error $\Delta f(x_k)$ (Equation~\ref{eq:clean_distance_metric}).
\item \textbf{Error Handling and Oracle Scoring:} If execution fails or times out, the run receives tiered penalty $\mathcal{F}_{\text{failure}}$ (Equation~\ref{eq:failure_penalties}), and the formatted traceback appends to $\mathcal{T}_{\text{feedback}}$. If execution completes, final coordinate $\mathbf{x}^*$ is evaluated on clean oracle $f_{\text{clean}}(\mathbf{x}^*)$ to assign scalar fitness $\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{A}_{\text{child}})$ via Equation~\ref{eq:meta_fitness_score}.
\item \textbf{Selection and Parent Resampling:} Child fitness $\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{A}_{\text{child}})$ is compared against incumbent score $\bar{\mathcal{F}}_{\text{parent}}$. If $\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{A}_{\text{child}}) > \bar{\mathcal{F}}_{\text{parent}}$, the child becomes the candidate parent. The incumbent is re-evaluated across $K = 3$ stochastic runs to update baseline $\bar{\mathcal{F}}_{\text{parent}}$ (Equation~\ref{eq:parent_resampling}), and the history archive $\mathcal{H}_{1 \dots g}$ logs the step.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Experimental Gauntlet}\label{sec:method_gauntlet}

The experimental protocol divides into two phases: prompt ablation and comparative baseline validation.

\textbf{Phase 1: Intra-Framework Prompt Ablation.}
Phase 1 tests the four prompt configurations (BASELINE, THINKING, VECTORIZATION, GUIDED) across five BBOB objective functions spanning distinct topological properties:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{$f_1$ (Sphere):} Unimodal separable surface for measuring convergence rate and gradient alignment.
\item \textbf{$f_8$ (Rosenbrock):} Non-separable surface with a curved parabolic valley, testing search through low-to-moderate conditioning.
\item \textbf{$f_{10}$ (Ellipsoidal):} Ill-conditioned unimodal surface with an extreme condition number ($10^6$), assessing anisotropic coordinate scaling.
\item \textbf{$f_{15}$ (Rastrigin):} Multimodal surface with a sinusoidal grid overlaying a global quadratic basin, testing local optima escape.
\item \textbf{$f_{21}$ (Gallagher 101 Peaks):} Asymmetric multimodal surface containing 101 randomly distributed Gaussian peaks, evaluating basin hopping without global gradient structure.
\end{itemize}
Runs execute under heteroscedastic noise regimes ($\text{CV} \in \{0.05, 0.10\}$) in dimension $D = 5$ to produce candidate optimizers for downstream testing.

\textbf{Phase 2: Comparative Benchmarking and Dimensional Transfer.}
Phase 2 benchmarks the top-performing synthesized algorithms against three established numerical optimizers: Covariance Matrix Adaptation Evolution Strategy (CMA-ES) \cite{Hansen2001}, Differential Evolution (DE) \cite{Storn1997}, and Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) \cite{Kennedy1995}.

The post-hoc benchmark evaluates two settings:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Deterministic Baseline Validation ($\text{CV} = 0.0$):} Checks whether heuristics evolved under noise maintain convergence rates on clean objective landscapes.
\item \textbf{Stochastic Robustness Validation ($\text{CV} > 0.0$):} Measures the performance degradation of standard baseline optimizers against synthesized heuristics as noise variance increases.
\end{enumerate}
To test structural generalizability, algorithms evolved in dimension $D = 5$ are evaluated directly on higher dimensions ($D \in \{10, 20\}$) without code modifications or parameter retraining.

\section{Evaluation Metrics}\label{sec:method_stats}

Benchmarking runs across $N_{\text{trials}} = 30$ independent random seeds for each problem instance, noise level, and search dimension. Performance is assessed using anytime convergence curves, Empirical Cumulative Distribution Functions (ECDFs), and non-parametric hypothesis testing.

\textbf{Anytime Convergence Trajectory Analysis.}
Optimization dynamics are logged by tracking the uncorrupted optimality gap at each evaluation step $k \in \{1 \dots B\}$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:clean_delta_step}
\Delta f(x_k) = \left| f_{\text{clean}}(x_k) - f(x^*) \right|
\end{equation}
The anytime performance metric records the best uncorrupted error found up to step $k$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:best_so_far_trajectory_eval}
\Delta f_{\text{best}}(k) = \min_{j \in \{1 \dots k\}} \left| f_{\text{clean}}(x_j) - f(x^*) \right|
\end{equation}
Across $N_{\text{trials}} = 30$ runs, performance is plotted as geometric mean trajectories with shaded standard error bands on logarithmic axes:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Horizontal Axis ($k$):} Total function evaluations on a logarithmic scale from $1$ to $10^5$.
\item \textbf{Vertical Axis ($\Delta f_{\text{best}}(k)$):} Uncorrupted optimality gap plotted across eight orders of magnitude from $10^2$ down to $10^{-8}$.
\item \textbf{Stagnation Tracking:} Horizontal plateauing marks the budget threshold where heuristics encounter premature convergence or variance collapse.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Empirical Cumulative Distribution Functions (ECDFs).}
To aggregate performance across different problem topographies without scale distortion, empirical cumulative distribution functions (ECDFs) are constructed over a range of target precision thresholds \cite{Hansen2021COCO}.

Let $\Theta$ define $M = 51$ logarithmically spaced precision thresholds spanning eight orders of magnitude:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:target_precision_spectrum}
\Theta = \left\{ 10^{\theta} \mid \theta \in [-8.0, 2.0], \, \Delta \theta = 0.2 \right\}
\end{equation}
For $N_{\text{trials}} = 30$ runs across problem instances $I$, the empirical attainment function at threshold $\tau \in \Theta$ and budget step $k$ is given by:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ecdf_formula}
F(k, \tau) = \frac{1}{|I| \cdot N_{\text{trials}}} \sum_{i \in I} \sum_{r=1}^{N_{\text{trials}}} \mathbb{I}\left[ \Delta f_{\text{best}}^{(i, r)}(k) \le \tau \right]
\end{equation}
where $\mathbb{I}[\cdot]$ is the indicator function:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:indicator_function}
\mathbb{I}\left[ \Delta f_{\text{best}}^{(i, r)}(k) \le \tau \right] =
\begin{cases}
1, & \text{if } \Delta f_{\text{best}}^{(i, r)}(k) \le \tau \\
0, & \text{if } \Delta f_{\text{best}}^{(i, r)}(k) > \tau
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
The Area Under the ECDF Curve ($\text{AUC-ECDF}$) integrates empirical attainment across logarithmic evaluation budgets:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:auc_ecdf}
\text{AUC-ECDF} = \frac{1}{|\Theta| \log_{10}(B)} \int_{1}^{B} \sum_{\tau \in \Theta} F(k, \tau) \, d(\log_{10} k)
\end{equation}
Higher $\text{AUC-ECDF}$ values denote faster convergence and higher solution rates across precision targets.
