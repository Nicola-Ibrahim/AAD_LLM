%==============================================================================
\chapter{Experiments}
\label{chap:experiments}
%==============================================================================

This chapter reports the available synthesis and benchmark snapshot.
Three model families have completed synthesis records. Champions are selected
from recorded synthesis candidates by ascending clean error, then evaluation
count, within each model--function--dimension--prompt--noise condition.
They are fixed before independent benchmarking; benchmark results do not
affect selection. Configuration defaults for future campaigns are distinguished
from settings recoverable from historical experiment records.

%==============================================================================
\section{Experimental Design}
\label{sec:experimental_design}
%==============================================================================

The labels \emph{implicit} and \emph{explicit} describe the information in
the synthesis prompt. A benchmark noise level instead describes the
objective observations received by an already generated optimizer during
independent evaluation. We report the synthesis and evaluation conditions
separately throughout the chapter.

\subsection{Benchmark Problems and Dimensions}
\label{subsec:experiment_problems}

The experiments use five functions from the noiseless BBOB suite
\cite{BBOB2009,Hansen2021COCO}. They were selected to cover different
optimization structures; the selection is not intended to represent the
entire suite.

\begin{table}[htbp]

    \label{tab:experiment_functions}
    \begin{tabular}{lll}
        \hline
        Function & Name & Main characteristic \\
        \hline
        $f_1$  & Sphere & Separable and unimodal \\
        $f_8$  & Rosenbrock original & Non-separable curved valley \\
        $f_{11}$ & Discus & Unimodal and highly conditioned \\
        $f_{15}$ & Rastrigin & Multimodal with global structure \\
        $f_{21}$ & Gallagher 101 peaks & Multimodal with weak global structure \\
        \hline
    \end{tabular}
\end{table}

In BBOB, $f_{11}$ is the highly conditioned Discus function, while the
separate $f_{13}$ function is Sharp Ridge. The selected $f_{21}$ function
contains 101 optima and belongs to the group with weak global structure
\cite{BBOB2009}.

Each selected function is evaluated at
\begin{equation}
    D \in \{2,3,5,10\}.
\label{eq:experiment_dimensions}
\end{equation}
Synthesis uses BBOB instance 1 for each function and dimension.
Independent benchmarking uses instance IDs 1--20, with one trial per instance.
Thus instance 1 is not held out; instances 2--20 provide additional landscape
realizations. Historical synthesis seeds are not stored in the supplied
database schema and cannot be recovered from its run IDs alone.

\subsection{Objective Noise}
\label{subsec:experiment_noise}

The benchmark includes a clean condition and stochastic conditions. In the
clean condition, a query returns $f_{\mathrm{clean}}(x)$. For stochastic
conditions, the optimizer receives
\begin{equation}
    f_{\mathrm{obs}}(x)
    = f_{\mathrm{clean}}(x)+\epsilon(x),
    \qquad
    \epsilon(x) \sim
    \mathcal{N}\!\left(0,
    \left[\sigma\left|f_{\mathrm{clean}}(x)-f^*\right|\right]^2\right),
\label{eq:experimental_noise_model}
\end{equation}
where $f^*$ is the known global optimum value for the relevant BBOB
instance. The noise standard deviation therefore depends on the clean
optimality gap: it decreases as the evaluated point approaches the optimum.
The noise levels considered are
\begin{equation}
    \sigma \in \{0,\,0.05,\,0.1,\,0.2\},
\label{eq:experiment_noise_levels}
\end{equation}
with $\sigma=0$ denoting clean evaluation. Each stochastic objective call
uses a new noise realization. The random-seed policy for the optimizer and
the noise generator is specified with the run matrix.
This is a repository-defined noise model applied to noiseless BBOB
functions through IOH; it is not the official COCO \texttt{bbob-noisy} suite.

Where noise levels vary during both synthesis and benchmarking, we use
$\sigma_{\mathrm{synth}}$ for synthesis and $\sigma_{\mathrm{eval}}$ for
independent evaluation. The cross-evaluation mappings are listed in
Table~\ref{tab:cross_evaluation_matrix}.

\subsection{Synthesis Conditions}
\label{subsec:synthesis_conditions}

Three LLM configurations are evaluated. Their model identifiers, quantization
artifacts, and recoverable inference settings are listed in
Table~\ref{tab:llm_configurations}.
These settings are kept separate from the prompt conditions in the analysis.

The variable content of the \texttt{TASK} prompt combines a common task
layout with one environment-information condition and one strategy
scaffold. The \texttt{EXAMPLE} and \texttt{FORMAT} prompts remain fixed.
The synthesis design therefore contains twelve \texttt{TASK} variants:
four scaffolds for each of the following three environment-information
conditions:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \emph{Implicit}: the prompt does not identify the objective as
    deterministic or stochastic;
    \item \emph{Explicit-Clean}: the prompt states that the objective is
    deterministic;
    \item \emph{Explicit-Noisy}: the prompt states that objective
    observations are stochastic.
\end{itemize}

The scaffolds are \emph{Baseline}, \emph{Thinking},
\emph{Vectorization}, and \emph{Guided}. The implicit condition therefore
has four variants, and the two explicit conditions have eight. The
Methodology chapter gives the prompt text; Table~\ref{tab:run_matrix}
records which variants were run with each model and problem setting.
The common example already requests NumPy vectorization. The Vectorization
condition therefore adds more specific guidance; it does not introduce
vectorization into an otherwise non-vectorized prompt.

\subsection{Synthesis Runs and Champion Selection}
\label{subsec:champion_selection}

Each synthesis run follows the LLaMEA procedure described in
Chapter~\ref{chap:methodology}. Candidate algorithms are generated and
evaluated within the run, and the evolutionary process maintains an
incumbent according to the clean evaluation rule defined there.

Under stochastic synthesis, noisy objective observations guide the
candidate optimizer's search and can affect the solution it returns. The
outer synthesis evaluator then assesses the returned $\texttt{best\_x}$
using the clean objective. Accordingly, stochastic observations can affect
which candidate is retained through their effect on the returned solution
and its clean score; the outer parent--child comparison is not made using
the noisy $\texttt{best\_y}$.

The export contains one selected candidate per synthesis condition, not one
champion per historical run. The selector ranks recorded candidates by
clean error and then evaluations used; exact ties retain input order.
Consequently, repeated synthesis records must not be counted as independent
champions in the benchmark. The three completed model families contribute
1,920 condition-specific champions in total.

Recorded synthesis budgets are 1,000,000 objective queries and maximum
iteration counts are 10 for every completed run. The current candidate
timeout is 30 seconds. The current configuration plans five future synthesis
repeats; the supplied database has no replicate/seed columns, so it does not
establish a five-repeat historical protocol. The snapshot contains 2,130
completed runs and 640 running DeepSeek-Coder-33B runs; no failed-status rows
are present. The unfinished model is excluded from the reported comparisons.
A completed session is not evidence that every candidate executed successfully.

\section{Independent Benchmark Evaluation}
\label{sec:independent_benchmark}
%==============================================================================

The benchmark uses a custom, evaluation-budget-limited protocol based on
IOH's implementation of selected BBOB functions. It follows COCO-inspired
principles, including evaluation-count-based effort, multiple problem
instances, and target-attainment analysis \cite{Hansen2021COCO}, but is not
a full standard COCO experiment. In particular, champions are selected for
specific synthesis conditions rather than applying one unchanged
general-purpose algorithm across the entire suite. The custom noise model
and terminal returned-point reliability analysis are study-specific.

The benchmark set consists of the selected champion for each covered
synthesis condition, together with the reference optimizers. No LLM
generation, algorithm modification, or synthesis feedback occurs during
this stage.

Each benchmark run is specified by the algorithm, BBOB function and
instance, dimension, evaluation noise level, objective-evaluation budget, and
random seed. The evaluation budget is $B_{\mathrm{eval}}=10{,}000D$:
20,000, 30,000, 50,000 and 100,000 clean objective evaluations for
$D=2,3,5,10$, respectively. Each condition targets $N_{\mathrm{runs}}=20$
trials, using instance $r$ and optimizer/noise seed $42+r$ for $r=1,\ldots,20$.
The generated champions have a 30-second execution timeout; the classical
baseline runners are called directly without an equivalent timeout wrapper.
This asymmetric execution constraint can affect comparisons and must not
be described as a uniform runtime limit. Solvers share the instance/seed schedule,
but consume random numbers according to their own search logic. The reference optimizers and their
implementation or parameter settings are listed in
Table~\ref{tab:reference_optimizers}.

The budget counts actual evaluations of the instrumented IOH objective,
including repeated evaluations of the same point. Once the budget is
exhausted, the wrapper returns the last observed value with a warning
instead of evaluating a new point. Consequently, the number of Python
objective-interface invocations can exceed $B_{\mathrm{eval}}$, even though
the recorded IOH evaluation count cannot. The initial search evaluation
consumes budget; the separate post-run clean assessment does not.
The framework does not restart a solver to spend unused budget. A solver
may stop early according to its own stopping rules or fail during execution;
20 instance trials must not be interpreted as 20 independent restarts on
one instance. Elapsed time is recorded separately, although timing boundaries
differ between adapters, so it is not used as the primary efficiency measure.

When $\sigma_{\mathrm{eval}}>0$, the optimizer receives only the
stochastic objective observations allowed by the evaluation environment.
After execution, the benchmark evaluator assesses the returned solution on
the clean objective. For a run $r$, the final clean optimality gap is
\begin{equation}
    \Delta f_r
    = \max\!\left(0,f_{\mathrm{clean}}(\mathbf{x}_{r,\mathrm{best}})-f^*\right),
\label{eq:benchmark_final_gap}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{x}_{r,\mathrm{best}}$ is the returned best point and $f^*$
is the global optimum value for that function instance. The optimizer does
not receive this post-run clean measurement. For $\sigma_{\mathrm{eval}}=0$,
the benchmark objective itself is clean.

The chapter reports final clean optimality gaps and target-attainment
results. It also includes convergence and empirical cumulative
distribution function (ECDF) plots where the recorded benchmark data
support them. For any anytime curve, the plotted quantity, sampling points,
and clean/noisy status of the trajectory are stated in its caption. A
clean final evaluation of $\mathbf{x}_{r,\mathrm{best}}$ alone does not
provide a clean trajectory; convergence plots based on clean gaps require
the corresponding evaluated-point history or clean checkpoints. The source is the clean IOH problem's logged evaluated-point history in
\path{results/ioh_traces}, with separate terminal measurements in
\texttt{provenance.json}. Query points are clean-scored before observational
noise is added. The separate returned-point evaluation is unlogged and does
not consume the search budget. Irregular trace entries are aligned by
last-observation carry-forward on a 300-point logarithmic grid from 1 to
$10^6$ for the exploratory profile figures. Median and quartiles summarize
the best clean queried gap, not necessarily the returned-point gap.
The supplied profile exports carry the final trace value beyond
$B_{\mathrm{eval}}$; this is visualization carry-forward, not additional
search or evidence of additional budget.

The primary solved target is $\Delta f\leq10^{-8}$; the secondary meaningful
convergence target is $\Delta f\leq10^{-2}$. Reliability is computed from
terminal returned-point errors. Each condition has a 95\% Wilson interval;
aggregate estimates average complete conditions with equal weight.
The tables below use 1,000 condition-cluster bootstrap resamples, seed
20260923, and percentile 95\% intervals. A condition, including all its 20
trials, is resampled as one unit. Incomplete or stale conditions are excluded,
not treated as failures; confirmed execution failures remain failures.
Runtime exceptions and timeouts are recorded with infinite terminal error
and therefore do not meet either success target. The current trial handler
does not retain separate timeout and runtime-exception categories in the
terminal provenance. This reliability measure thus includes execution
failure under the implemented constraints, not only search performance.

The available statistical engine implements condition-wise Kruskal--Wallis
omnibus tests, two-sided Mann--Whitney $U$ comparisons, Vargha--Delaney
$A_{12}$ for minimization, and Benjamini--Hochberg FDR correction at
$\alpha=0.05$ over valid pairwise tests in one invocation.
These are unpaired tests despite the shared instance/seed schedule.
No pairwise or omnibus numerical report is present in the supplied outputs,
so this chapter reports descriptive contrasts and bootstrap uncertainty,
not unverified adjusted $p$-values. Current installed IOH and LLaMEA versions
are 0.3.22 and 1.2.0; dependency versions are not persisted per historical run.

conditions used for synthesis. The planned mappings are listed in
Table~\ref{tab:cross_evaluation_matrix}. A problem is described as
\emph{unseen} only when its function, dimension, or evaluation environment
was excluded from the corresponding synthesis run.

\section{Benchmark Results}
\label{sec:benchmark_results}
%==============================================================================

The results below compare champions from implicit and explicit synthesis
and then examine their performance when benchmark conditions change. The full native condition table is
\path{results/reports/native_reliability_v1.csv}; terminal trial records and
clean query traces are under \path{results/ioh_traces}.
The export contains 1,920 native condition rows, of which 1,890 have 20
completed trials. One otherwise complete row has a stale code hash:
LLM-3, Explicit-Noisy/vectorization, $f_{11}$, $D=3$, $\sigma=0.05$.
After checking exported champions against code and provenance, 1,889 complete
current conditions (37,780 trials) enter the descriptive summaries.
No raw-gap mean is used for the headline comparison: fixed-target success
probabilities are averaged equally across eligible conditions.
The exploratory profile exports do contain an arithmetic mean of
function-level convergence summaries in their overall panel. That raw-gap
panel is scale-dependent and is not used for cross-function ranking.
Adaptive-target ECDFs use 51 logarithmic targets derived from observed
positive gaps separately at each noise level; they are exploratory and
are not directly comparable to the fixed-target solved probability.

%==============================================================================
\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:experiment_discussion}
%==============================================================================

The results show heterogeneous behavior rather than uniform improvement
with model size or additional guidance. The common-strategy implicit comparison
does not favor the largest Qwen model, while its Explicit-Clean strategy
ablations include stronger solved rates. Explicit native noisy rates are
generally lower than clean-origin rates, but those contrasts combine changes
in synthesis and evaluation noise. Frozen-champion robustness is the more
appropriate design for isolating an evaluation-noise change.

The primary reliability statement is about repeatable performance of a
selected program across benchmark instances, not the probability that an LLM
will independently discover a successful program. Historical synthesis
repeat counts are unbalanced and seeds are unrecorded. Incomplete conditions
and the stale-hash exclusion limit coverage; descriptive rankings may change
after those conditions are completed. No statistical-significance claim
is made without exported tests. The illustrative profile figures show only
LLM-2 at $D=2$; the condition tables cover all tested dimensions and models.

The 30-second timeout on generated champions, without an equivalent
baseline timeout, limits a purely evaluation-count-based comparison.
Timeout failures cannot be distinguished from other execution exceptions
in the current terminal export. The study therefore measures reliability
under the implemented execution constraints, rather than an unrestricted
COCO comparison. No timeout-policy change or reevaluation is assumed here.

The interpretation of stochastic synthesis distinguishes the optimizer's
internal search from evolutionary selection. Noisy observations affect the
candidate optimizer's trajectory and can change its returned solution. The
outer synthesis procedure compares candidates using the clean evaluation
of their returned solutions. The benchmark stage then measures the frozen
champions under its own evaluation conditions without changing them. These
stages should not be described as if noisy $\texttt{best\_y}$ values
directly determined parent--child selection.
