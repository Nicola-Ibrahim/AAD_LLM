\chapter{Background and Related Work}\label{chap:research}

To properly contextualize the operational environment of this research, it is necessary to examine the foundational concepts of continuous black-box optimization alongside the mathematical vulnerabilities introduced by stochastic noise. Building upon these theoretical foundations, recent advancements in Large Language Models (LLMs) have driven a paradigm shift toward Automated Algorithm Design (AAD), enabling autonomous agents to synthesize complete, executable search strategies. This chapter establishes the mathematical background of continuous optimization under uncertainty, details the failure mechanics of classical heuristics in noisy fitness spaces, surveys state-of-the-art literature in LLM-driven code evolution and prompt scaffolding, and isolates our primary research gap.

%-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Theoretical Background}\label{sec:bg_theory}

Continuous black-box optimization focuses on identifying the global minimum of an unknown, real-valued objective function:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bbob_min}
    \min_{x \in \mathcal{S} \subseteq \mathbb{R}^D} f(x)
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{S}$ represents a bounded $D$-dimensional continuous search space (typically $[-5, 5]^D$), and the analytical gradient $\nabla f(x)$—alongside any internal structural equations governing the objective—is completely inaccessible. In these unconstrained or box-constrained settings, point sampling queries constitute the sole mechanism for information retrieval. In standard literature, algorithms designed for these problems are systematically benchmarked against the Black-Box Optimization Benchmark (BBOB) suite \cite{Hansen2010}. Standardized within the IOHexperimenter \cite{deNobel2024} and COCO platforms, the BBOB testbed comprises 24 synthetic mathematical functions designed to emulate distinct topological challenges encountered in complex physical engineering. As detailed in Table~\ref{tab:bbob_groups}, these 24 benchmark functions are categorized into five structural problem groups based on their landscape properties.

Crucially, the BBOB framework standardized the use of empirical cumulative distribution functions (ECDFs) to characterize algorithm performance, measuring runtime by the number of function evaluations required to achieve a specific target precision delta ($\Delta f_t = f(x) - f(x^*) \le 10^{-8}$). Over recent decades, human engineering has yielded robust heuristics to navigate these deterministic spaces, most notably the Nelder-Mead simplex algorithm \cite{Nelder1965}, Differential Evolution (DE) \cite{Storn1997}, Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) \cite{Kennedy1995}, and the Covariance Matrix Adaptation Evolution Strategy (CMA-ES) \cite{Hansen2001}. Empirical benchmarks on the noiseless BBOB testbed consistently demonstrate that CMA-ES represents the state-of-the-art baseline for continuous optimization, outperforming local search methods like NEWUOA \cite{Powell2006} by multiple orders of magnitude on highly conditioned, non-separable topologies due to its robust covariance matrix update mechanics \cite{Hansen2010}.

To overcome the manual trial-and-error bottleneck of inventing metaheuristics, researchers introduced modular algorithm design frameworks \cite{vanRijn2016, deNobel2021}. Modular CMA-ES \cite{vanRijn2016} and Modular DE \cite{deNobel2021} decompose classical algorithms into discrete, configurable blocks (e.g., active covariance updates, mirrored sampling, or adaptive step-size scaling). While these frameworks established combinatorial search spaces encompassing millions of candidate algorithm configurations, they remain strictly bounded by human imagination: an automated configurator can only recombine module options explicitly programmed into the framework by human domain experts.

While human-engineered heuristics achieve high precision on deterministic benchmarks, real-world physical systems—such as battery parameter identification \cite{Plett2004, Xiong2018} and structural finite-element updating \cite{Mottershead1993, Marwala2010}—are inherently non-deterministic. Physical objective evaluations derived from telemetry or hardware sensors are persistently corrupted by thermal noise, electromagnetic interference, and quantization limits. As established in the foundational taxonomy by Jin and Branke \cite{Jin2005}, stochastic uncertainty in optimization can disrupt the parameter space (variable deviations post-optimization), the time space (dynamic topological drift), the surrogate space (approximation errors), or the fitness space. This research focuses on uncertainty acting within the fitness space, where the true time-invariant objective height $f_{\text{clean}}(x)$ is corrupted by an additive stochastic perturbation $z$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:noisy_evaluation_bg}
    f_{\text{noisy}}(x) = f_{\text{clean}}(x) + z
\end{equation}
rendering the observed fitness a random variable centered around the true underlying fitness \cite{Jin2005}. In continuous optimization testbeds, sensory disruptions are commonly simulated via three statistical distributions \cite{Hansen2009Noisy}:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Additive Uniform Noise:} Models digital sensor quantization and truncation errors: $z \sim \mathcal{U}(-a, a)$.
    \item \textbf{Additive Cauchy Noise:} A heavy-tailed distribution modeling severe, intermittent hardware spikes or packet dropouts: $p(z) = (\pi \gamma [1 + (z/\gamma)^2])^{-1}$.
    \item \textbf{Additive Gaussian Noise:} Models background thermal and electronic white noise operating independently of signal height \cite{Hansen2009Noisy}: $z \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)$.
\end{enumerate}

The mathematical failure modes of direct search methods under noise were isolated by Arnold and Beyer \cite{Arnold2003}. Evaluating local search heuristics on noisy sphere landscapes ($f(x) + z$), their analysis proved that pattern search, Nelder-Mead, and implicit filtering inevitably suffer from \textit{step-size stagnation}. When operating at fine spatial resolutions, true objective differentials $\Delta f_{\text{clean}}$ drop below the noise standard deviation $\sigma$. Misinterpreting these stochastic perturbations as local topological features, direct search heuristics prematurely contract their step sizes, exploding the noise-to-signal ratio and permanently freezing progress far from $f(x^*)$ \cite{Arnold2003}. Conversely, Arnold and Beyer demonstrated that multi-parent evolution strategies $(\mu/\mu, \lambda)\text{-ES}$ maintain stochastic linear convergence by leveraging global intermediate recombination to achieve \textit{genetic repair}—averaging out zero-mean noise across parents to sustain a $\mu$-fold larger mutation step length \cite{Arnold2003}.

In population-based algorithms like CMA-ES \cite{Hansen2001}, distribution updates depend directly on sorting candidate solutions by relative fitness rank. The mean vector $m^{(g+1)}$ for generation $g+1$ is computed as:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cma_mean_update_bg}
    m^{(g+1)} = m^{(g)} + \sum_{i=1}^{\mu} w_i \left( x_{i:\lambda} - m^{(g)} \right)
\end{equation}
where $w_1 \ge w_2 \ge \dots \ge w_\mu > 0$ represent recombination weights assigned according to sorted performance $f(x_{1:\lambda}) \le \dots \le f(x_{\lambda:\lambda})$. When fitness evaluations are corrupted by noise $z$, the empirical rank ordering is inverted ($f_{\text{noisy}}(x_{i:\lambda}) > f_{\text{noisy}}(x_{j:\lambda})$ despite true $f_{\text{clean}}(x_i) < f_{\text{clean}}(x_j)$). This rank corruption misallocates dominant recombination weights to sub-optimal vectors, distorting both step-size adaptation $\sigma^{(g)}$ and covariance matrix accumulation $C^{(g)}$ \cite{Jin2005, Hansen2009Noisy, Sarafian2024}. Jin and Branke confirmed that classical human-engineered workarounds—such as static sample averaging or expanding population sizes—severely degrade optimization efficiency due to the $\sqrt{N}$ sample size penalty \cite{Jin2005}. Furthermore, static pre-runtime hyperparameter tuning cannot resolve this trade-off, as fixed parameter selections cannot dynamically adapt to local noise variance shifts across the search space. This creates an active mandate to investigate whether autonomous logic generators can synthesize structural alternatives rather than relying on static computational averages.

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related_work}

To eliminate human design bottlenecks, early Automated Algorithm Design (AAD) frameworks relied on Genetic Programming \cite{Burke2013} or hyper-heuristics. However, the immense combinatorial breadth of programmatic space and the rigid constraints of predefined syntax trees frequently restricted true algorithmic discovery. This barrier was recently broken by integrating Large Language Models (LLMs), validated for strict mathematical discovery by Romera-Paredes et al. \cite{Romera2023}. Their framework, FunSearch, pairs a generative model with a systematic evaluator to evolve code skeletons, successfully discovering new lower bounds for the cap-set problem and synthesizing novel bin-packing heuristics \cite{Romera2023}.

A comprehensive survey by Zhang et al. \cite{ReflectiveSurvey2025} formally categorizes the operational paradigms of LLM-assisted evolutionary computation into three distinct prompt operators:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Selection Operators:} Prompts that instruct the language model to evaluate and rank programmatic candidates based on empirical performance logs.
    \item \textbf{Variation Operators:} Semantic mutation and crossover prompts where the language model refactors, mutates, or recombines existing code structures to discover novel algorithmic logic.
    \item \textbf{Reflective Operators:} Closed-loop feedback prompts that inject execution tracebacks, compiler error logs, and quantitative performance deltas back into the context window, driving self-correction.
\end{enumerate}

The EvoLLM framework \cite{Lange2024} established that LLMs can act directly as evolutionary numerical samplers to predict candidate coordinate vectors. However, EvoLLM lacks code interpretability, incurs heavy token costs, and remains restricted to 5-dimensional instances \cite{Lange2024}. Conversely, frameworks such as Evolution of Heuristics (EoH) \cite{Liu2024}, Algorithm Evolution using LLMs (AEL) \cite{AEL2024}, and Reflective Evolution (ReEvo) \cite{ReEvo2024} restrict the LLM's role to generating reusable code blocks. While these frameworks outcompeted classical baselines, their empirical validations remained confined to discrete combinatorial tasks like the Traveling Salesperson Problem (TSP) \cite{Liu2024, ReEvo2024}.

In the continuous optimization domain, recent zero-shot paradigms such as AutoEP \cite{AutoEP2025} explore leveraging Exploratory Landscape Analysis (ELA) features as prompt priors to guide LLM code generation. While AutoEP demonstrates that structural landscape features accelerate heuristic convergence, its feedback mechanism relies on static ELA pre-processing on clean functions \cite{AutoEP2025}. Parallel advancements in multi-agent structures, including Reflexion \cite{Shinn2023} and automated self-debugging architectures \cite{Chen2023}, confirmed that passing text-based execution traces and compiler error tracebacks back to the generative model drastically accelerates code convergence. Chen et al. proved that instructing a model to perform self-debugging using execution tracebacks boosts functional accuracy by up to 12\% while matching the performance of baselines demanding a $10\times$ higher sampling budget \cite{Chen2023}.

While foundational AAD frameworks rely heavily on static initial system instructions, recent research emphasizes the critical role of prompt scaffolding and automated prompt evolution. To address the problem of manual, trial-and-error prompt construction, frameworks such as EvoPrompt \cite{Guo2023} connect LLMs directly with Evolutionary Algorithms to optimize discrete prompt strings using crossover and mutation operators. Guo et al. demonstrated that automatically evolved prompts significantly outperform static human-engineered instructions across language reasoning and classification benchmarks, establishing prompt strings themselves as key evolutionary variables \cite{Guo2023}. 

In the domain of programmatic code generation, providing structural scaffolding—such as Abstract Syntax Tree (AST) feature extraction and Explainable AI (XAI) feedback utilized in LLaMEA-SAGE \cite{LLaMEASAGE2026}—has been proven to effectively bias LLM-driven variation operators. By augmenting reflective prompts with explicit code structural metrics rather than relying solely on raw numerical fitness scores, LLaMEA-SAGE significantly accelerates the discovery of high-performing continuous optimization heuristics \cite{LLaMEASAGE2026}. These findings demonstrate that prompt scaffolding directly governs an LLM's search trajectory through programmatic space. However, both EvoPrompt \cite{Guo2023} and LLaMEA-SAGE \cite{LLaMEASAGE2026} evaluate prompt scaffolding exclusively under deterministic, noise-free objective evaluations.

Building directly upon executable code generation, self-debugging, and reflective prompt scaffolding, this thesis extends the Large Language Model Evolutionary Algorithm (LLaMEA) framework \cite{LLaMEA2024}. Engineered specifically to automate the discovery of continuous black-box optimization heuristics, LLaMEA structures an LLM within a $(1+1)$ Evolution Strategy. The framework iteratively prompts the model to generate complete, reusable Python classes, executes that code against target mathematical problems via the IOHexperimenter benchmarking suite, and subsequently feeds both runtime error tracebacks and aggregated performance metrics—specifically the Area Over the Convergence Curve (AOCC)—back into the LLM's context window to drive self-debugging and refinement %
. The scalar AOCC metric evaluated over budget $B$ on target precision $\Delta f_t$ is defined as:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:aocc_metric_bg}
    \text{AOCC} = \frac{1}{B} \sum_{k=1}^{B} \left( 1 - \frac{\log_{10}(\max(\Delta f(x_k), 10^{-8}))}{\log_{10}(\Delta f_{\text{init}})} \right)
\end{equation}

Empirically, foundational experiments proved that LLaMEA-generated algorithms successfully discovered novel heuristic logic that outcompeted state-of-the-art metaheuristics, including vanilla CMA-ES and Differential Evolution, on the 5-dimensional continuous BBOB suite, while maintaining highly competitive generalizability when zero-shot scaled to unseen 10-dimensional and 20-dimensional instances \cite{LLaMEA2024} %
. Recent extensions to the framework, such as LLaMEA-BO \cite{LLaMEABO2025}, have successfully demonstrated LLaMEA's capacity to synthesize Bayesian Optimization components and surrogate-assisted search logic for computationally expensive black-box functions.

However, evaluating a $(1+1)$ Evolution Strategy within a non-deterministic evaluation sandbox introduces the \textit{Ghost Parent} failure mode. If a poorly designed child algorithm receives an unusually favorable negative noise draw ($z \ll 0$), its recorded performance metric becomes artificially inflated. Under strict elitist selection, this over-evaluated child replaces the parent and becomes an un-killable statistical ghost parent. Subsequent, structurally superior offspring are repeatedly rejected because they cannot match the ghost parent's mathematically unachievable baseline score.

Currently, all existing state-of-the-art AAD frameworks—including FunSearch \cite{Romera2023}, EvoPrompt \cite{Guo2023}, EoH \cite{Liu2024}, ReEvo \cite{ReEvo2024}, AutoEP \cite{AutoEP2025}, LLaMEA-SAGE \cite{LLaMEASAGE2026}, LLaMEA \cite{LLaMEA2024}, and LLaMEA-BO \cite{LLaMEABO2025}—operate strictly under deterministic, noiseless objective functions. It remains entirely unknown whether closed-loop evolutionary pressure within an LLM context window can compel a foundation model to discover specialized uncertainty-mitigation mechanics (e.g., dynamic sampling, momentum filtering, or adaptive step-size regularization), how different prompt scaffolding strategies (e.g., minimal operational instructions versus explicit declarations of landscape stochasticity) influence the synthesis of these mechanics, whether cost-effective open-weights language models possess sufficient programmatic reasoning to discover these strategies compared to commercial baselines, and whether algorithms evolved in stochastic sandboxes achieve cross-environment generalizability when deployed back onto unseen noisy or deterministic landscapes. This thesis directly targets this unaddressed boundary layer.
