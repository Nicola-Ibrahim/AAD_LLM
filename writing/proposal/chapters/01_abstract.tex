\chapter*{Summary}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Summary}

Continuous black-box optimization plays an important role in engineering
applications, where objective evaluations may be affected by stochastic
noise. Such noise can obscure differences between candidate solutions
and mislead search decisions. Established noise-handling methods can
reduce these effects, but designing and adapting optimization algorithms
for different problems still requires substantial expertise. This thesis
proposal investigates Automated Algorithm Design (AAD) using Large
Language Models (LLMs) to generate and refine executable optimization
algorithms. Building on the Large Language Model Evolutionary Algorithm
(LLaMEA) framework, the proposed study asks whether evolutionary feedback
from noisy fitness evaluations can lead LLMs to synthesize heuristics
that perform reliably under stochastic conditions. Controlled noise will
be applied to continuous black-box benchmark functions to provide a
repeatable evaluation setting. Algorithms generated under noisy and
noiseless conditions will be evaluated across different noise settings,
including clean evaluations, to compare their performance, robustness,
and generalization. Established optimization methods may also be included
as reference baselines. The study will provide an empirical assessment
of how exposure to noise during algorithm generation affects the
resulting optimization heuristics.
