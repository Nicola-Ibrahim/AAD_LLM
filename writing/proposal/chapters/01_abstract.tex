\chapter*{Proposal Summary}

\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Proposal Summary}

Continuous black-box optimization plays an important role in parameter extraction and complex engineering simulations, yet traditional human-engineered metaheuristics can perform poorly when operating in non-deterministic environments. In real-world applications, empirical evaluations are frequently affected by stochastic noise that obscures the underlying optimization landscape, introducing deceptive local optima and statistical anomalies that can mislead conventional gradient-free search methods. Historically, addressing these challenges has required human experts to develop specialized mechanisms, such as filtering or step-size adaptation rules, through extensive manual experimentation, making the process labor-intensive and difficult to scale across structurally diverse optimization landscapes. To address this limitation, this thesis investigates a systematic framework for Automated Algorithm Design (AAD) using Large Language Models (LLMs), moving from static parameter tuning toward the autonomous synthesis of executable algorithmic code. Building on the Large Language Model Evolutionary Algorithm (LLaMEA) framework, the proposed approach establishes a closed-loop evolutionary system in which a generative model is evaluated under controlled non-deterministic conditions. By exposing generated heuristics to a specialized noise distribution during evaluation, the study investigates whether the LLM can adapt its programmatic logic and discover mechanisms for handling uncertainty. The research culminates in an empirical benchmark comparing noise-specialized heuristics with equivalent heuristics evolved under clean, deterministic conditions, providing a quantitative assessment of the LLM's ability to develop optimization strategies that generalize across environments under uncertainty.
