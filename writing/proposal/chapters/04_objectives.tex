\chapter{Research Objectives}
\label{chap:objectives}

The primary aim of this thesis is to investigate the capacity of Large
Language Models (LLMs) to autonomously synthesize optimization heuristics
that can handle stochastic fitness evaluations. The proposed research
examines Automated Algorithm Design (AAD) under noisy conditions through
three objectives and their corresponding research questions.

\section*{Objective 1: Formulating a Stochastic Evaluation Sandbox}

The first objective is to extend continuous benchmark functions with
controlled noise to investigate the behavior of LLM-generated optimization
algorithms under stochastic evaluations. It addresses the following
question:

\begin{quote}
\textbf{RQ1: How can continuous optimization benchmarks be systematically
extended to simulate stochastic landscapes?}
\end{quote}

The proposed approach is to develop a wrapper layer over functions from
the noiseless BBOB suite. Established benchmark definitions and literature
on optimization under uncertainty will guide the introduction of
controlled statistical noise
\cite{Hansen2009Noiseless,Hansen2009Noisy,Jin2005}.
The resulting environment will provide a repeatable setting for evaluating
generated algorithms using noisy fitness observations. Applying noise
to the same underlying BBOB functions, rather than using the separate
noisy-BBOB suite, will allow a direct comparison of AAD performance
under noiseless and noisy evaluations.

\section*{Objective 2: Autonomous Synthesis of Noise-Resilient Heuristics}

Existing work has demonstrated that LLaMEA can generate competitive
optimization algorithms for noiseless benchmark functions
\cite{LLaMEA2024}. The second objective is to investigate whether the
same evolutionary code-generation process can produce heuristics that
handle noisy fitness evaluations. It addresses the following question:

\begin{quote}
\textbf{RQ2: Can Large Language Models autonomously synthesize optimization
heuristics that are resilient to noise?}
\end{quote}

The LLaMEA framework will be applied to a representative set of continuous
optimization landscapes using lightweight and cost-effective language
models. The experiments will examine whether repeated evolutionary
feedback from noisy evaluations leads generated Python optimization
classes to incorporate mechanisms for handling uncertainty. Changes in
the generated algorithms over successive generations will be analyzed
alongside their performance to assess whether noise-handling strategies
emerge and whether they are effective in the tested conditions.

\section*{Objective 3: Empirical Benchmarking and Cross-Environment Analysis}

The third objective is to evaluate the generated algorithms under different
noise conditions and examine their performance, robustness, and
generalization. It addresses the following comparative question:

\begin{quote}
\textbf{RQ3: How robust are LLM-generated heuristics across different noise
conditions compared with established optimization methods?}
\end{quote}

Algorithms generated under noisy and noiseless conditions will be evaluated
across different noise settings, including noiseless evaluations. This
comparison will examine whether exposure to noise during algorithm
evolution leads to differences in performance, robustness, or
generalization.
